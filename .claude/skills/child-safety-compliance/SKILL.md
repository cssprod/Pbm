# Child safety and compliance

This is not legal advice. Platform rules and laws change; verify against the current official pages at least each quarter and record the date and links in `brand/compliance-log.md`. When something is unclear, choose the safer option and flag it for the owner.

## 1. COPPA and YouTube "Made for Kids"

- The content is directed at children under 13, so under US COPPA and YouTube's terms **every video and the channel must be set to "Made for Kids" (MFK)**. Mislabelling can bring FTC penalties.
- Consequences to plan around (confirm on YouTube help): no personalised ads (lower ad revenue), comments disabled, no notification bell, no cards or end screens, no community posts or stories, no channel memberships, no merch shelf, no live chat. Build the business plan around these (see `business-strategy`).
- Never collect personal information from children: no "comment your name", no "send us your drawings with your address", no contests requiring child data, no links to sign-up forms aimed at kids.
- Other regions: the UK Age-Appropriate Design Code, EU/GDPR-K rules, and similar laws may apply. Keep the same "collect nothing from kids" posture everywhere.

## 2. YouTube kids quality and monetisation

YouTube rewards kids' content that encourages being a good person, learning and curiosity, creativity and play, and connecting with the real world, and can demonetise low-quality kids' content (e.g. heavily commercial, encouraging bad behaviour, or misleading). Our premise fits well; keep it that way:

- Each episode has a real educational or values payoff.
- No clickbait thumbnails or titles that misrepresent the video.
- Avoid "mass-produced", repetitive or template-only output; YouTube's monetisation policies penalise inauthentic, repetitious content. Every episode needs an original story and meaningful human creative input. This matters a lot for an AI-assisted channel.

## 3. AI disclosure

- Use the platforms' AI/synthetic-content disclosure settings when they apply. Animated, clearly unrealistic content may not require it, but disclose anyway in the description ("Animated with the help of AI tools; written and reviewed by people"). Transparency builds parent trust.
- Keep C2PA/content credentials metadata where tools provide it.

## 4. TikTok and Instagram

- Both require users to be 13+. Accounts are owned and run by the adult owner and aimed at **parents and families**.
- Captions address parents ("A 45-second safety lesson to practise with your kids").
- Never invite children to follow, comment, DM, or duet. Turn off duets/stitches/DMs from strangers if there is any sign kids are engaging; moderate comments daily and hide anything inappropriate.
- Don't use the owner's son's face, name, school, or location. If the family ever appears, the owner decides, and location data and identifying details are removed.

## 5. Advertising and sponsors

- Ads to kids are tightly regulated (e.g. US CARU guidelines, FTC endorsement rules). No product placement aimed at children without clear disclosure, no "ask your parents to buy". Sponsors should be family brands, and messaging is directed at parents.
- Use the platform's paid-promotion disclosure checkbox for any sponsored content.

## 6. Content red lines (automatic QA fail)

- Graphic violence, blood, weapons used on people, realistic peril to children, horror imagery.
- Martial arts used to bully, retaliate, or solve arguments.
- Dangerous stunts kids could copy (jumping off furniture, kicking near others, breaking boards at home).
- Body-safety content that is frightening or blames the child.
- Real children's likeness, voices, or identifying details.
- Stereotypes or mockery of Korean or any other culture; political content.
- Scary jump-scares, flashing lights (photosensitivity), sudden loud sounds.
- Any link, QR code, or call to action that leads a child off-platform.

## 7. Safety lessons

Self-defense content requires instructor sign-off (see `self-defense-lessons`) and the standard disclaimer.

## 8. Escalation

If any comment, message, or account interaction suggests a child is at risk, do not reply; hide it, record it, and notify the owner immediately so they can report it through the platform (and to authorities where appropriate).
