# Rights and licensing

Not legal advice. For anything with real money or a dispute, the owner should consult an IP attorney. Keep every licence and proof in `brand/licences.md` (asset, source, licence type, date, link or file).

## Show name and brand

- Before launch, search the USPTO trademark database (and other target markets), YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, app stores, and domains for the show name and character names. Record results in `brand/trademark-search.md`.
- If clear and the show gains traction, consider registering the name and logo (classes for entertainment and merchandise). Secure matching handles and a domain early.

## ITF, federations, and General Choi

- "ITF" and federation names and logos are claimed by one or more organisations (the ITF has split into several federations). **Do not use any federation logo, emblem, or the ITF tree logo**, and do not imply endorsement.
- Describing the content as "ITF-style Taekwon-Do" and crediting the teachings of General Choi Hong Hi is factual description; keep it factual.
- Pattern names, tenets, belt meanings, and history are facts and ideas, not copyrightable. But **text from the Encyclopedia of Taekwon-Do or other books must not be copied verbatim** beyond short attributed quotes; retell in our own words. The student oath and tenets may be quoted with attribution.
- Images or photos of General Choi: don't use photos without a licence. A respectful stylised illustration is preferable.

## AI-generated assets

- Use only tools whose terms grant commercial use of outputs on the plan you pay for. Save a copy/link of the terms on the date of use.
- Pure AI output may have limited copyright protection (e.g. US Copyright Office guidance requires human authorship). Protect the show by keeping human creative work central and documented: scripts, character designs, story, edit decisions. Keep drafts and records.
- Don't prompt with living artists' names or copyrighted characters. Don't imitate existing shows' characters or logos.

## Music, voice, fonts, SFX

- Original commissions: get a written work-for-hire or exclusive licence assignment.
- Library assets: licence must cover YouTube monetisation, social platforms, and (if planned) merchandise or ads.
- Voice actors: written agreement covering use, duration, and any AI use of their voice (explicit consent required for synthetic voice).
- Fonts: commercial video licence.

## Partners and schools

- Schools that share our clips: a simple written permission (they may post unedited clips with credit; no paid ads without agreement).
- Instructors who review content: credit them (with permission) and agree in writing on any fees.

## Our IP

- Copyright notice in descriptions: "© <year> <owner/company>".
- If others re-upload our videos, use YouTube's copyright tools; decide with the owner before filing takedowns.
