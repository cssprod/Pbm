# The Five Tenets and the Student Oath

These are the moral heart of the series. Every episode should spotlight **one tenet**. The tenet pairs naturally with the pattern hero of that episode (suggested pairings are in `patterns.md` and `patterns.json`).

## The Five Tenets (Taekwon-Do Jungshin 태권도 정신)

| # | Tenet | Korean (ITF romanization) | Hangul | Kid meaning | Everyday example for kids |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Courtesy** | Ye Ui | 예의 | Be polite and respectful to everyone. | Bow to your instructor, say please and thank you, wait your turn, be kind to the new kid. |
| 2 | **Integrity** | Yom Chi | 염치 | Know right from wrong, and do the right thing even when nobody is watching. Feel bad when you've done wrong, and fix it. | Tell the truth about breaking the vase. Don't cheat on a game. Don't take credit for someone else's work. |
| 3 | **Perseverance** | In Nae | 인내 | Keep trying, be patient, never give up. | Practising a kick 100 times. Doing homework when it's hard. Waiting patiently. |
| 4 | **Self-Control** | Guk Gi | 극기 | Be the boss of your body, your words and your feelings. | Not hitting back when angry. Controlling your kicks in sparring. Stopping screen time when asked. |
| 5 | **Indomitable Spirit** | Baekjul Boolgool | 백절불굴 | A spirit that can't be broken. Be brave and stand up for what's right, even when you're scared or outnumbered. | Standing up for a friend being bullied (by getting a grown-up and using words). Trying again after losing. |

### How General Choi described them (paraphrased from the *Encyclopedia*)

- **Courtesy**: students should practise elements of courtesy to build noble character and conduct training in an orderly manner: promote a spirit of mutual concession, be ashamed of vice and contemptuous of others' wrongs, be polite to one another, encourage a sense of justice and humanity, and distinguish instructor from student, senior from junior.
- **Integrity**: being able to define right and wrong and having the conscience, if wrong, to feel guilt. His examples include an instructor who misrepresents himself, a student who fixes a breaking demonstration, and a student who requests a rank from an instructor.
- **Perseverance**: "Patience leads to virtue or merit," and "one can make a peaceful home by being patient for 100 times." Rising to a goal through repetition and patience. He quotes Confucius.
- **Self-Control**: extremely important inside and outside the dojang, whether sparring or in personal affairs. "A loss of self-control in free sparring can prove disastrous." He quotes Lao-Tzu: the stronger is the one who wins over oneself, not over someone else.
- **Indomitable Spirit**: shown when a courageous person and their principles are pitted against overwhelming odds. He cites the 300 Spartans at Thermopylae, and says a serious student will at all times be modest and honest, and when confronted with injustice will deal with the belligerent without fear or hesitation, whoever and however many they may be. He quotes Confucius: "It is an act of cowardice to fail to speak out against injustice."

> Note for writers: the tenet text above is a paraphrase. Wording varies slightly between editions and schools. Don't present any of it as an exact quotation unless you check it against the printed *Encyclopedia* first.

### Kid-friendly mnemonic

"**C**ool **I**nstructors **P**ractise **S**uper **I**ntensely" (Courtesy, Integrity, Perseverance, Self-Control, Indomitable Spirit). This one is made up for the show. Schools use many different ones.

### Story hooks per tenet (one-liners for episode ideas)

- **Courtesy**: the hero bows to a rival who was rude, and the rival becomes a friend.
- **Integrity**: the hero finds a gold coin in the dojang and returns it (ties in with General Choi Yong, "look upon gold as stone").
- **Perseverance**: Dan-Gun's bear waits 100 days in the cave while the tiger gives up.
- **Self-Control**: Ul-Ji Moon Dok wins with a clever poem instead of rushing into battle.
- **Indomitable Spirit**: Admiral Yi with 13 ships against 133.

## The Student Oath (학생 선서)

Recited at the start or end of class. Great as the series' recurring chant (the kid cast can recite it in the opening or closing).

> **I shall observe the tenets of Taekwon-Do.**
> **I shall respect the instructor and seniors.**
> **I shall never misuse Taekwon-Do.**
> **I shall be a champion of freedom and justice.**
> **I shall build a more peaceful world.**

### Kid translation (for an on-screen "what does it mean?" segment)

1. I will live by the five tenets.
2. I will listen to and respect my teacher and the older students.
3. I will never use Taekwon-Do to hurt, bully or show off. Only to protect.
4. I will stand up for what's fair and right.
5. I will help make the world a calmer, kinder place.

## Other ITF character teachings worth using

- **Training secrets / ideals.** General Choi listed ideals such as: study the theory of power thoroughly, understand the purpose and method of each movement, make eyes, hands, feet and breath act in one coordinated action, and practise with the goal of perfection.
- **"Taekwon-Do and morality."** The *Encyclopedia* contains a long section on moral culture: humility, filial piety (respect for parents), loyalty, and the duty of the strong to protect the weak.
- **The kihap (기합).** A short, sharp shout that focuses breath and energy. Kid version: "your spirit's voice". It doubles as a self-defense tool (see `self-defense-lessons.md`).
