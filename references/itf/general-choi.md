# General Choi Hong Hi (최홍희), Founder of ITF Taekwon-Do

> Spelling note: the founder's name is **Choi Hong Hi** (최홍희). It is sometimes misremembered as "Choi Jung Hi". Always use *Choi Hong Hi* on screen and in captions.

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Born | 9 November 1918, Hwa Dae, Myong Chun district, Hamgyong province (now North Korea) |
| Died | 15 June 2002, Pyongyang |
| Known as | "The Father of Taekwon-Do" |
| Named the art | **Taekwon-Do** ("the way of the foot and fist"), name approved 11 April 1955 |
| Founded | International Taekwon-Do Federation (ITF), 22 March 1966, Seoul |
| Key book | *Encyclopedia of Taekwon-Do* (15 volumes, 1983), and the one-volume *Condensed Encyclopedia* |
| Rank | Major General, Republic of Korea Army; 9th degree black belt |

## The kid-friendly story (usable as the series' origin tale)

**The small boy who wouldn't give up.** Hong Hi was a thin, sickly child, so small that his father worried about him. But he had a big, stubborn spirit. When he was about 12, he stood up for what he believed was right at school and was sent away. His father then sent him to study calligraphy (beautiful brush writing) with a teacher named **Han Il Dong**, who was also a master of **Taekkyon**, the old Korean foot-fighting game. The teacher saw how frail the boy was and taught him Taekkyon to make him strong. Lesson for kids: *being small is not the same as being weak.*

**Learning far from home.** As a young man he travelled to Japan to study. There he trained hard in Karate and earned a black belt. He combined what he learned with the kicks of Taekkyon and his own ideas about science and movement.

**Brave in hard times.** During World War II, Korea was ruled by Japan and young Korean men were forced into the Japanese army. Hong Hi was put in prison for supporting Korean independence. While in prison he kept training, alone in his cell, and even taught his cellmates and a guard. (For the show: the lesson is that even when your world gets very small, you can keep your mind and body strong.)

**Building a new art.** After Korea was freed in 1945, he became an officer in the new Korean army. He taught his soldiers his martial art, and his unit, the **29th Infantry Division** on Jeju Island, became the home where Taekwon-Do grew up. That's why the pattern Hwa-Rang has 29 movements. In 1955 a group of masters agreed on the name he proposed: **Taekwon-Do**.

**Sharing it with the world.** He led demonstration teams around the world, founded the ITF in 1966, and spent the rest of his life travelling to teach. He created the 24 patterns (tul), naming each after a hero or event from Korean history so that students would learn the story of Korea every time they trained.

**Why 24 patterns?** In General Choi's words (paraphrased from the *Encyclopedia*): a human life of maybe 100 years is only like one day compared with eternity, so he left 24 patterns, one for each hour of the day, as a symbol of a whole life. *This is a great "ending narration" line for the series.*

## His philosophy in kid language

General Choi wrote that Taekwon-Do is not mainly about fighting. It is about building a person who is:
- **Kind and polite** (Courtesy)
- **Honest** (Integrity)
- **Patient and never quitting** (Perseverance)
- **In charge of their own body and feelings** (Self-Control)
- **Brave enough to stand up for what's right, even when scared** (Indomitable Spirit)

He also wrote that the strong should protect the weak, and that a Taekwon-Do student should help build a more peaceful world (the last line of the Student Oath).

Useful quote ideas (paraphrased, safe for kids):
- "Taekwon-Do is a way of life."
- "Be willing to go where the going may be tough, and do the things that are worth doing even though they are difficult." (from his list of ideals for students)
- "The strong should protect the weak."

## Topics to avoid or handle carefully (brand safety)

These are real parts of the history. For a 6-10 audience they should be **left out** or kept very light. Adult-facing content (parents' notes, about pages) can mention them neutrally.

1. **Korean politics and the split of Taekwon-Do.** General Choi fell out with the South Korean government in the early 1970s and moved the ITF to Canada (1972). The South Korean government then backed a separate organization (the Kukkiwon / WTF, now World Taekwondo, the Olympic style). Later he introduced Taekwon-Do to North Korea. Do not take sides; do not mention North vs South conflict in episodes.
2. **Juche pattern.** Juche was added in the 1980s and is named after North Korea's state ideology. Many ITF-style schools still teach the older pattern **Ko-Dang** in that slot instead. Recommendation for this channel: tell the **Ko-Dang** story (a peaceful independence hero), and mention Juche only as "some schools learn Juche" in a parent note. See `patterns.md`.
3. **ITF has split into several groups** after his death (e.g. ITF headquartered in Vienna, ITF in Spain/Benidorm, ITF in Pyongyang, and other federations such as GTF, UTF). Avoid naming one "official" ITF. Say "ITF-style Taekwon-Do" or "General Choi's Taekwon-Do".
4. **War and violence in the hero stories.** Many pattern namesakes were generals or freedom fighters. Focus on their character (bravery, cleverness, loyalty, peace), not on killing. One namesake (An Joong-Gun) is famous for an assassination: never show it. See the guidance in `patterns.md`.
5. **Japan-Korea history.** Present the occupation era simply as "a hard time when Korea was not free", without making any present-day people or nation the bad guys.
6. **Disputed details.** Some dates and claims in the official pattern definitions differ from mainstream history. `patterns.md` flags these. Use the official ITF definition when quoting the pattern meaning, and keep extra historical claims to what's flagged as safe.
