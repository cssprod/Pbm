# Sources

Codes (S1, S2...) are cited at the bottom of each reference file.

**How this was compiled:** the official pattern, belt and tenet definitions are close paraphrases of the standard ITF texts that students memorise, which are reproduced on many school and federation sites (S6–S8). Historical background comes from standard references on each figure (S10–S13). Online sources were located in November 2026. Before you quote anything word for word on screen, check it against a printed copy of S1 or S2. Where the ITF text and mainstream history disagree, `patterns.md` flags it under "History check".

## Primary ITF texts
- **S1.** Choi Hong Hi. *Encyclopedia of Taekwon-Do* (15 volumes). International Taekwon-Do Federation, 1983 (later editions 1987, 1993). Volumes 8–15 hold the pattern definitions; Volume 1 holds history, philosophy, tenets, oath and belt meanings.
- **S2.** Choi Hong Hi. *Taekwon-Do: The Korean Art of Self-Defence* (the "Condensed Encyclopedia"). ITF, various editions 1965–2008 (5th edition 1999 is common).
- **S3.** Choi Hong Hi. *Taekwon-Do and I* (autobiography, 3 volumes, in Korean; partially translated). Source for his childhood, Han Il Dong and Taekkyon, prison, and the 29th Division.
- **S4.** ITF (Vienna) official site, philosophy, tenets and oath pages: https://www.itf-tkd.org *(check which ITF group's wording you follow)*.
- **S5.** The Student Oath and Tenets as reproduced in standard ITF grading syllabi (e.g. S6, S8).

## Secondary: pattern and belt definitions
- **S6.** ITF Taekwondo (UK site), "Belt Colour Meanings": https://www.itftaekwondo.com/belt-colour-meanings/ and "ITF patterns": https://itftaekwondo.com/patterns/
- **S7.** Taekwondo Wiki (Fandom), "ITF Patterns": https://taekwondo.fandom.com/wiki/ITF_Patterns
- **S8.** Do TaeKwon-Do, pattern pages (e.g. Do-San): https://dotaekwondo.net/itf/patterns/5/do-san.html. Also ITKD (New Zealand), "7 patterns guide" (PDF): https://www.members.itkd.co.nz/reference/essays/7-patterns-guide.pdf
- **S8b.** Wikipedia, "International Taekwon-Do Federation" (history, splits, pattern list): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Taekwon-Do_Federation

## Myth and history
- **S9.** Il-yeon. *Samguk Yusa* (Memorabilia of the Three Kingdoms), c. 1281. Source of the Dan-Gun founding myth and the Manpasikjeok (Moon-Moo) flute legend. English: Ha Tae-Hung & Grafton Mintz, trans., Yonsei University Press, 1972.
- **S10.** Kim Bu-sik. *Samguk Sagi* (History of the Three Kingdoms), 1145. Source for Kim Yu-Sin, Gyebaek, Eulji Mundeok (and his poem), Gwanggaeto, Munmu, and the Hwarang.
- **S11.** Encyclopedia of Korean Culture (Academy of Korean Studies): https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr (for each figure: Ahn Chang-Ho, Yi I, Yi Hwang, Jeong Mong-ju, Choi Yeong, Kim Deok-ryeong, Seosan Daesa, Son Byong-hi, Cho Man-sik).
- **S12.** Wikipedia biographies used to cross-check dates: Choi Hong Hi; Dangun; Ahn Changho; Wonhyo; Yi I; Shin Saimdang; An Jung-geun; Yi Hwang; Hwarang; Yi Sun-sin; Battle of Myeongnyang; Turtle ship; Gwanggaeto the Great; Gwanggaeto Stele; Jeong Mong-ju; Gyebaek; Son Byong-hi; Bang Jeong-hwan (Children's Day); Kim Deok-ryeong; Cho Man-sik; March 1st Movement; Yu Gwan-sun; Kim Yu-sin; Choe Yeong; Yeon Gaesomun; Siege of Ansi; Eulji Mundeok; Battle of Salsu; Munmu of Silla; Daewangam; Manpasikjeok; Hyujeong (Seosan); Sejong the Great; Hangul; Jang Yeong-sil.
- **S13.** Michael J. Seth. *A Concise History of Korea* (Rowman & Littlefield, 3rd ed. 2020). Background for the eras.
